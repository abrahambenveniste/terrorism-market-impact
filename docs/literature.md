# Prior research and proposed contribution

This is a short orientation list, not a systematic literature review. It helps define a researchable question and identify methods to compare. Before making a novelty claim, search databases such as Google Scholar, Crossref, and EconLit systematically and document the search date, terms, and inclusion criteria.

## Relevant cross-country studies

- Drakos, K. (2010). [Terrorism activity, investor sentiment, and stock returns](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rfe.2010.01.001). *Review of Financial Economics*, 19(3), 128–135. Studies daily returns across 22 countries using flexible world-market return specifications and conditional volatility methods.
- Ahmed, T., Hussain, S., Akbar, M., & Rehman, A. U. (2022). [Impact of terrorism on stock market: Evidence from developed and developing markets](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2022.102786). *International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction*, 70, 102786. Uses a multi-country sample and examines event characteristics such as casualties, location, and attack features.
- MacKinlay, A. C. (1997). [Event Studies in Economics and Finance](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.35.1.13). *Journal of Economic Literature*, 35(1), 13–39. A foundational review of event-study design and inference.
- Kolari, J. W., & Pynnönen, S. (2010). [Event Study Testing with Cross-sectional Correlation of Abnormal Returns](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhq072). *The Review of Financial Studies*, 23(11), 3996–4025. Explains why same-date events and correlated markets can make naive event-study tests overstate significance.

## What this project could add

The proposed question is whether the market response magnitude itself changes over time, not only whether attacks are associated with returns on average. It also emphasizes a transparent, country-by-country audit of event definitions, market coverage, trading calendars, and reuse rights. These are potential contributions, not established gaps: prior work may already address parts of this question, and the feasible country/date sample may be narrower than planned.

The seven requested markets include mature and emerging markets, different index methodologies and exchange schedules, and Ukraine's exceptional wartime disruptions. A pooled trend therefore needs country effects, severity/event-composition controls, source-phase sensitivity, and separate country plots. It should not be presented as a universal investor response without those checks.

## Literature review questions for the next pass

1. Which papers already estimate changes in terrorism-related abnormal returns across decades, rather than a pooled average response?
2. Which event definitions do those papers use, and how do they compare with GTD's non-state terrorism criteria?
3. Which papers use country indices versus individual stocks, and which market-model benchmarks and variance corrections do they use?
4. Are there published analyses of the requested countries that can serve as a replication or calibration set?
